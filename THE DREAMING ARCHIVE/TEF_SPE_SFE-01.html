Snatching frame — core concept and today’s variant

Title: Snatching Frame — 26×40 Core Concept + PET/PMMA Bevel-Block Variant (v1)

Lineage / context:

  • Family: press support tools designed to snatch rather than stretch.

  • This entry: first formal description of the snatching frame, plus today’s PET/PMMA reinforced version.

1. Core concept

  • Purpose:

    • Create a support frame for an etching press that converts the press action into a sudden downward “snatch” on paper and plate, instead of a gradual stretch.

    • Improve registration and reduce paper distortion under pressure.

  • Basic geometry (26×40 format):

    • Designed for 26×40 cm paper; plates must be smaller and can be held by an inner removable recess.

    • Frame built from three laser-cut “plywood” layers (approx. 3 mm each):

      • Base layer:

        • Single 2×26 cm recess, positioned 3 cm from the edge on all sides.

        • This is the pre-press lock: first contact zone before the main paper window engages.

      • Middle layer:

        • Same 2×26 cm recess.

        • Plus a 26×40 cm window, starting 2 cm further in.

        • This creates a small bridge between pre-press recess and main window.

      • Top layer:

        • One large excision defining the lid footprint: pre-press zone + gap + 26×40 window.

  • Lid idea (original):

    • A lid with two extrusions:

      • One matching the 2×26 cm pre-press recess.

      • One matching the 26×40 cm paper window.

    • Intended sequence:

      • Lid first engages in the pre-press recess (“pre-press lock-in”).

      • Then continues into the main 26×40 window as the press advances.

    • Concern: in practice, the pre-press recess is very close to the main window, so misalignment under press load might cause binding rather than smooth sliding.

2. Material realities and issues noticed

  • The “plywood” is actually IKEA-back-panel-type board:

    • Strong enough, but bendy and prone to falling apart rather than cleanly snapping.

    • Laser-cut edges are neat and blackened, but long thin spans and small extrusions feel fragile.

  • Observed fragilities / doubts:

    • Lid extrusions in this material dent easily.

    • The short 2 cm bridge between pre-press recess and 26×40 window looks delicate (though supported from below).

    • The pre-press lock is so close to the main window that it may not give enough “runway” for the lid to self-correct under press pressure.

3. Reinforcement strategy — PMMA and solvent adhesive

  • Adhesive concept:

    • Use a home-made adhesive: acetone + PMMA (or melted packing foam).

    • This mix wicks into the porous board and cures as a rigid acrylic composite, strengthening the frame.

  • PMMA inserts:

    • Inner PMMA elements for:

      • The pre-press recess.

      • The 26×40 lid area.

    • PMMA is slightly thinner than the board, leaving room for plate + paper.

    • These inserts act as a stiff inner skeleton and precise contact surfaces.

4. PET base and lid — today’s PET variant

  • PET stock:

    • Cheap, strong desktop protector PET sheets, laser-cut into large rectangles.

    • Laser is grumpy with PET but acceptable for big, simple shapes (window open, exhaust on, no hovering).

  • PET base:

    • A PET sheet under the frame, extending beyond it:

      • Front extension: currently planning 3–4 cm as a runway before the frame hits the rollers.

      • Back extension: 11 cm behind the frame as a hardware / hinge zone.

      • Total length (current plan): 65 cm.

    • 4× 4 mm holes, 3 cm from the back edge, for screws/rivets/Chicago fasteners.

    • Function:

      • Provide a smooth entry surface for the press.

      • Support the frame and distribute load.

      • Keep all hardware out of the press zone.

  • PET lid (flexible cover):

    • A second PET sheet, same length as the base (≈65 cm).

    • Attached/hinged at the back extension (outside press zone) using nylon rivets, Chicago screws, etc.

    • Can be folded up to place plate and paper, then folded down over the frame.

  • Front bevel-block on PET lid:

    • A thin block (less thick than the frame) attached to the underside of the PET lid at the front.

    • The inner face of this block is bevelled (the “gesture bevel”).

    • When the lid is lowered:

      • The bevel meets the front edge of the frame.

      • Because the block is thinner, it slides downward along the bevel, naturally seating behind the front edge.

      • This holds the lid in place without clips or rails.

    • This is the only bevel that truly matters: it ensures the lid “falls” into correct position before entering the press.

5. Operational gesture (today’s imagined use)

  1. PET base + frame + PMMA inserts are assembled and aligned.

  2. PET lid is attached at the back extension, able to flex up and down.

  3. To print:

    • Fold PET lid up.

    • Place plate(s) within the 26×40 window; add paper.

    • Fold PET lid down; the bevel-block finds and falls over the front edge of the frame.

  4. Feed the PET runway into the press:

    • Rollers first engage PET, then the frame, then the snatching zone.

    • The lid holds the paper/plate stack in place; PMMA stiffens; PET base supports.

  5. Press passes over the assembly, producing a snatching rather than stretching action.

6. Open questions / next tests

  • Final decision between 3 cm vs 4 cm front extension after seeing it physically.

  • Long-term behavior of the acetone + PMMA/foam adhesive (warping? brittleness?).

  • Durability of the bevel-block under repeated passes.

  • Whether future PET stock with a thicker molded front edge could replace the glued block entirely.
