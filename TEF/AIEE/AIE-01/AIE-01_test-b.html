Acrylic Ink Experiment 01
Test B

AIE‑01 TEST B — OFFICIAL TEF SUMMARY

Lineage: AIEE — Acrylic Ink Engineering Experiments
Test: AIE‑01 Test B
Status: Breakthrough
Tag: aiee-tackyglue-breakthrough

1. Purpose of the Test

To evaluate the behaviour of the AIE‑01 Test A ink across multiple substrates, compare it with standard lino ink, and identify modifications that improve:

  • roller adhesion

  • surface transfer

  • internal cohesion

  • film formation

2. Substrates Tested

  • Floor vinyl plate — dark, low‑energy, difficult surface; used as control/torture test.

  • FSEP — Fusion‑Sealed Excision Plate.

  • Floor vinyl with protected excisions.

  • Floor vinyl with unprotected excisions.

3. Standard Lino Ink — Control Observations

  • First bottle produced inconsistent drops of pure ink.

  • Second bottle was more fluid and transparent.

  • Weak transfer to floor vinyl; colour showed through the dark surface.

  • Confirms floor vinyl as a poor adhesion surface and therefore a reliable control.

Conclusion: Standard lino ink performs weakly on floor vinyl but establishes the baseline.

4. AIE‑01 Test A Ink (Unmodified)

Observed behaviours:

  • Large roller tracks.

  • Uneven roller coating.

  • Ink repeatedly re‑adhered to the roller (“roller pull‑back”).

  • Pull‑back occurred with all water‑based inks tested.

  • Only primer ink (not tested here) is known to resist pull‑back.

On floor vinyl (protected or unprotected):

  • Ink refused to stay on the surface.

  • Residue formed at edges.

  • Surface wetting was poor.

Conclusion: Ink lacked internal cohesion and surface wetting ability.

5. Modification 1 — Green Calcium Carbonate

  • Added ~1 teaspoon.

  • Slight improvement in transfer.

  • Better results when pressing by hand.

  • Still insufficient.

Conclusion: Calcium carbonate increases body but does not solve cohesion or tack.

6. Modification 2 — Tacky Glue (Breakthrough)

Added ~1.5 tablespoons of tacky glue.

Immediate transformation:

  • Ink became cohesive.

  • Behaved like lino ink.

  • Adhered to the roller properly.

  • No more roller pull‑back.

  • Formed continuous films.

  • Transferred reliably to floor vinyl and FSEP.

  • Did not stick to paper.

  • Produced solid colour fields.

  • Worked even with unprotected excisions.

  • Two contrasting plates successfully printed.

  • Only hand pressure used.

Conclusion: Tacky glue is the breakthrough binder for AIEE inks. It provides cohesion, tack, film formation, and roller adhesion.

7. Final Assessment

AIE‑01 Test B produced the first viable AIEE ink capable of:

  • behaving like traditional lino ink

  • adhering to rollers

  • transferring to difficult surfaces

  • forming continuous films

  • printing on FSEPs

  • producing solid colour fields

  • functioning with unprotected excisions

This test marks the turning point of the Acrylic Ink Engineering lineage.
